01
Entry Protocol

Eligibility & Team

  1. Only registered college students may participate.
  2. A team can contain a maximum of three members, including the team leader.
  3. Participants must compete only with the members listed during registration. Member substitutions require organizer approval.
  4. Every participant must carry a valid college ID card. A clear photograph or digital copy of the ID card is accepted if the original is unavailable.
  5. Teams must report before the announced reporting time. Late entry may reduce the available build time.
02
Equipment

What You Must Bring

  1. Every team must bring at least one working laptop suitable for development and demonstration.
  2. Bring the required laptop chargers, adapters, cables and any special hardware needed for your solution.
  3. Wi-Fi and charging facilities will be provided at the venue. Because these are shared facilities, keep an offline copy of important tools and dependencies.
  4. Participants are responsible for their devices, accounts, files and personal belongings. Organizers are not responsible for damage or data loss.
03
Originality Check

Build During the Hackathon

  1. Teams must not submit an already completed, previously published or previously presented project.
  2. The main application and its core features must be developed during the hackathon period.
  3. Teams may plan an idea beforehand, but they must not arrive with a finished solution.
  4. Public libraries, frameworks, APIs, SDKs, open-source packages, UI components and AI development tools are allowed when their use is disclosed.
  5. Judges may inspect source files, repository history and timestamps, or ask technical questions to confirm that the work belongs to the team.
  6. Plagiarism, copying another team, false claims or hiding pre-built work may result in immediate disqualification.
04
Expected Output

A Prototype Is Accepted

The final project does not need to be production-ready. A convincing working prototype is enough.

  1. The prototype should demonstrate the main problem, solution and essential user flow.
  2. Mock data, simulated hardware responses or limited features are acceptable when the team clearly identifies what is simulated.
  3. At least one important feature should work during the final demonstration.
  4. Teams must honestly explain incomplete features, technical limitations and planned improvements.
  5. A concept presentation without a working demonstration may receive fewer functionality marks.
05
Final Evaluation

How to Present to the Judges

01Problem

Explain the real problem and affected users.

02Solution

Show how your idea solves it differently.

03Prototype

Demonstrate the core working flow.

04Technology

Explain the stack, architecture and onsite work.

05Impact

Describe feasibility, scalability and future scope.

06
Code of Conduct

Fair Play & Safety

  1. Behave respectfully toward other teams, volunteers, faculty and judges.
  2. Do not access another participant's device, account, source code or data.
  3. Projects must not contain illegal, discriminatory, offensive or unsafe content.
  4. Follow all venue, network, electrical and safety instructions.
  5. The judges' decision is final. Organizers may clarify rules when required for fair conduct.
